DroneSplat — Process & Paper Explainer

3D Gaussian Splatting for robust 3D reconstruction from in-the-wild drone imagery. This site walks through what we ran, the paper’s method, and lets you explore the reconstructions live in 3D.

Jiadong Tang, Yu Gao, Dianyi Yang, Liqi Yan, Yufeng Yue, Yi Yang — Beijing Institute of Technology & Hangzhou Dianzi University

01Overview

DroneSplat tackles two core problems when reconstructing 3D scenes from real-world drone photos: moving objects (cars, people) that break the “static scene” assumption, and limited viewpoints (some areas are seen by only a few photos).

The key idea

  • Adaptive Local-Global Masking — hides dynamic-object pixels from the loss, with a threshold that adapts automatically.
  • Voxel-guided 3DGS — initialize from multi-view stereo (DUSt3R) + optimization constrained by a voxel grid, keeping geometry accurate when views are sparse.

What this site contains

  • The full pipeline we ran on 2 scenes: Simingshan & Sculpture.
  • An explanation of the paper’s method, with the key equations.
  • Live, draggable 3D viewers — orbit & 360° — embedded below.

05The problems the paper solves

① Dynamic scenes (distractors)

Moving objects violate the multi-view consistency assumption of 3DGS/NeRF → artifacts & floaters.

Prior methods need predefined classes (can’t tell a parked car from a moving one) or use a fixed threshold throughout training.

② Limited viewpoints

Some areas are captured by only ~3 photos → radiance fields overfit the inputs and look poor at novel views.

InstantSplat adds MVS priors but no constraint on later optimization, so the prior is wasted.

06Method ① — Adaptive Local-Global Masking

Identify dynamic-distractor pixels and hide them from the loss — without predefined classes, and with a threshold that adapts per scene & training stage.

3DGS loss with mask:  L = (1−λ)·M·LL1 + λ·M·LD-SSIM

Object-wise residuals → adaptive local threshold

SAM2 gives a mask per object; all pixels of one object share one average residual. The threshold comes from the frame’s residual statistics (mean + variance), relaxed early in training:

TL(t) = E[R] + Var[R]·( 1 + λL · (Tmax−t)/Tmax )

Complement global masking (SAM2 video tracking)

A car stopped at a red light can slip past the local mask. A stricter global threshold flags any object ever caught moving; 5 prompt points feed SAM2’s video tracking to mask it across all frames.

TG = E[R] + λG·Var[R]  |  Final mask: M(t) = Mlocal(t) ∪ Mglobal(t)
Intuition: local = “what looks dynamic now”; global = “objects ever caught moving, tracked everywhere.”

07Method ② — Voxel-guided Gaussian Splatting

Accurate geometry under limited views, exploiting MVS priors without letting 3DGS run away.

Geometric-aware point sampling

DUSt3R’s dense cloud is filtered: space is split into N voxels; each point is scored by confidence × FPFH geometric feature; only the top-k per voxel initialize Gaussians.

Score(p) = Conf(p) · F̃PFH(p)  → keep top-k points per voxel

Voxel-guided optimization

Each Gaussian is bound to its voxel; if it strays past τ× the voxel size its gradient decays, and splits/clones only go into empty voxels. Junk voxels (too few Gaussians, or low opacity) are pruned.

Hyperparameters (paper): λL=0.4λG=2.8N=80 k=3τ=3.5γ₁=0.003 γ₂=2γ₃=0.0757,000 iters · A100 masking @ iter 500

10Beyond the repo — re-implementing the missing pieces

We verified that the public GitHub code is a partial implementation: only Adaptive Local Masking ships; the Complement Global Masking and Voxel-guided optimization — two of the paper’s four contributions — are absent (no voxel/FPFH/video-tracking anywhere in the repo; densification is stock 3DGS). That is the main reason our reconstructions still showed faint “ghosts”.

So we re-implemented the two missing components from the paper’s equations, each behind a flag:

① Complement Global Masking

Objects whose residual exceeds TG are tracked across all frames with SAM2’s video predictor (center + 4 edge prompts), catching distractors that are momentarily static. New global_masking.py + --use_global_mask.

②b Voxel-guided optimization

Gaussians are bound to a voxel grid; strayed ones get gradient-decayed, and voxels whose mean opacity is low are pruned — removing faint ghost Gaussians. Added to gaussian_model.py + --use_voxel.

Honest takeaway: a real, visible ghost reduction — but incremental on Simingshan, since its distractors are mostly moving cars the local mask already handled. PSNR is essentially unchanged (23.2 vs 23.4) because the removed ghosts partly overlap distractors still present in the test GT. The 3D Orbit viewer above now loads this improved Simingshan model. Fully matching the paper would still need the DUSt3R dense initialisation (②a) and the authors’ exact evaluation protocol.
